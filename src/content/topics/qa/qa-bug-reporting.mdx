## Why bug quality is a skill

A vague ticket wastes engineering time. Strong reports shrink cycle time and reduce “cannot reproduce” ping-pong.

## Mental model

<Callout type="mental-model">
  Every actionable report answers: what broke, where, for whom, how to reproduce, what should happen, how bad it is, and what evidence proves it.
</Callout>

## Template that engineers trust

1. **Summary** — One line with user impact (“Checkout pay button no-ops on Safari iOS”).
2. **Environment** — App version, OS/browser, account type, locale, feature flags.
3. **Steps** — Numbered, starting from a clean known state.
4. **Expected vs actual** — Specific, observable outcomes.
5. **Evidence** — Screenshot, video, HAR, Playwright trace, correlation/request id.
6. **Severity / priority suggestion** — Impact vs business urgency (they can diverge).
7. **Notes** — Workarounds, related tickets, first-seen build.

## Severity vs priority

- **Severity** — How badly the product is wrong (data loss > cosmetic).
- **Priority** — What we fix first (CEO demo tomorrow may elevate a medium severity).

## Common mistakes

- Mixing multiple unrelated bugs in one ticket.
- Skipping setup (“log in as usual”).
- Omitting expected results.
- Filing without checking duplicates or recent deploys.

## Interview angle

<Callout type="interview">
  Rewrite into an actionable ticket: “Checkout broken sometimes on mobile.”
</Callout>

## Practice checklist

- [ ] Create a team bug form from the template above.
- [ ] Attach a Playwright trace or HAR once.
- [ ] Classify severity vs priority for five historical bugs.
