## Why progressive delivery exists

“Stop the world and hope” deploys amplify risk. Blue/green and canary reduce blast radius by controlling **how traffic meets a new version**.

## Mental model

<Callout type="mental-model">
  Blue/green: two complete environments; flip a router when green is healthy. Canary: send a small % of traffic (or users) to the new version and expand if signals stay green.
</Callout>

```text
Blue/green:   users --> LB --100%--> blue(v1)   | green(v2) waiting
After cutover: users --> LB --100%--> green(v2)

Canary:       users --> LB --95%--> v1
                           --5% --> v2  (watch error rate / latency)
```

## When to choose which

- **Blue/green** — Need fast full rollback; schema changes must be carefully dual-compatible; infra cost of two stacks is acceptable.
- **Canary** — Want real production signal before full exposure; good observability and automated abort rules exist.
- **Neither alone** — Feature flags can decouple *code deploy* from *feature exposure*.

## Common mistakes

- **Canary without metrics** — You are gambling slowly.
- **Incompatible DB migrations** — Neither strategy saves a one-way destructive migration.
- **Manual “looks fine” gates** — Prefer SLO-based auto-rollback.

## Interview angle

<Callout type="interview">
  When would you pick blue/green over canary for a NestJS API behind a load balancer?
</Callout>
