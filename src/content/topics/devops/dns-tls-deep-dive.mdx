## Why DNS and TLS sit on the critical path

Users never see your perfect deploy if the name points wrong or the certificate chain fails validation. Most “site down” incidents start at DNS TTL or TLS expiry.

## Mental model

<Callout type="mental-model">
  DNS maps names → answers (A/AAAA/CNAME/MX/TXT). TLS authenticates the server (and optionally client) and negotiates confidentiality. Certificates bind a public key to names via a trusted CA chain.
</Callout>

```bash
dig +short example.com A
dig example.com NS
openssl s_client -connect example.com:443 -servername example.com </dev/null 2>/dev/null | openssl x509 -noout -dates -subject
```

Know CAA, ACME automation, and that CNAME at apex usually needs ALIAS/ANAME provider features.

## Common mistakes

- **Low awareness of TTL** — Long TTLs delay failover; zero TTL hammers resolvers.
- **Missing SAN names** — Cert for `example.com` but clients hit `www`.
- **Incomplete chain** — Mobile clients fail while desktop browsers forgive.

## Interview angle

<Callout type="interview">
  Walk through diagnosing “NET::ERR_CERT_DATE_INVALID” after a certificate renewal.
</Callout>

## Practice checklist

- [ ] Trace resolution with `dig` and compare authoritative vs recursive answers.
- [ ] Inspect a live cert chain with OpenSSL.
- [ ] Sketch DNS cutover with TTL planning.
