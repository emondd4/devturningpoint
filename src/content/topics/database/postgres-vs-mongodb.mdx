## Why “Mongo for speed, Postgres for seriousness” is a caricature

Both systems are mature. The decision hinges on data shape, transaction needs, query patterns, team skills, and operational constraints—not blog memes.

## Mental model

<Callout type="mental-model">
  Start from access patterns and invariants. Prefer Postgres when joins, constraints, and multi-row transactions dominate. Prefer MongoDB when document locality and flexible hierarchies dominate—and you accept different consistency/ops tradeoffs. Consider Postgres jsonb before adding a second database.
</Callout>

Decision prompts:

1. Do you need multi-document ACID often?
2. Is your schema boundary stable or wildly polymorphic?
3. Who will own backups, upgrades, and indexing expertise?
4. Can one system serve 80% of queries without heroic workarounds?

## Common mistakes

- **Two databases by default** — Dual-write consistency nightmares.
- **Ignoring jsonb middle path** — Postgres can store documents when appropriate.
- **Choosing for resume keywords** — Production owns you afterward.

## Interview angle

<Callout type="interview">
  An e-commerce catalog has nested variants and strict payment transactions. How do you split storage—if at all?
</Callout>

## Practice checklist

- [ ] List top 10 queries for your domain.
- [ ] Mark which need joins/constraints.
- [ ] Decide single-store vs polyglot with explicit sync rules.
