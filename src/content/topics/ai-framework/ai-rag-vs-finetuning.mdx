<Callout type="mental-model">
RAG changes what the model can see; fine-tuning changes how it tends to behave. Most product knowledge problems start as RAG, not fine-tuning.
</Callout>

## Decision question

Do you need **up-to-date or private facts** in answers, or do you need **stable style/format/behavior** that prompting cannot reliably get?

## Prefer RAG when

- Knowledge changes often (docs, policies, tickets).
- You need **citations** and auditability.
- Data must stay in your store (privacy / tenancy).
- You lack a large labeled fine-tune set.

## Prefer fine-tuning (or continued training) when

- You need consistent tone, schema adherence, or domain phrasing after prompts plateau.
- Tasks are narrow and labeled data is strong.
- Latency/cost of huge prompts is worse than a specialized model—**measure first**.

## Often combine

RAG for facts + light fine-tune/prompt distillation for format. Evaluate both layers separately.

## Cost / latency / security notes

- RAG: embedding + context tokens; watch injection via docs.
- Fine-tune: training cost, eval burden, model ops; watch data leakage into weights.
- Prefer local/open experiments before paid fine-tunes; warn before any billable training job.

## Checklist

- [ ] Can a citation-based RAG prototype meet the bar?
- [ ] Do you have a versioned eval set for both approaches?
- [ ] Who owns freshness: index updates or retraining cadence?
- [ ] What is the rollback plan?
